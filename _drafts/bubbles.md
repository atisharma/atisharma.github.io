


## Bubbles

A similar logic applies to bubbles. An incipient bubble often gets going with high returns based on a good fundamental reason, which attracts speculation with borrowed money. But, by definition, they reach a point where the unusually high (usually accelerating) returns are fuelled primarily by leveraged bets *chasing* returns pushing up the price and causing the same high returns to continue. This requires exponentially growing amounts of (borrowed) capital. This feedback loop is the essence of a bubble and flips into reverse when there is insufficient lending available to support the continued growth rate.

Is it rational to invest in a bubble?

Ignore day-to-day volatility for a moment and consider the risk of a crash.
Assume that we can estimate a probability of the bubble bursting in the next period $$t$$, which we call $$p_c(t)$$.
Let the growth rate be $$r(t)$$.
In the case of a crash, we can recover the fundamental value, $$v(t)$$.

The amount at risk (the "stake") is then $$(1 - v)$$.

The (binary) Kelly bet is then

$$
    f(t) = 1 - p_c - \frac{p_c (1-v)}{r}
$$
